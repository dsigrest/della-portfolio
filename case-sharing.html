Sharing & Embeds

Senior Product Designer
Growth — Top of Funnel
Oct 2022 – May 2023
iOS, Android, Web

Designing a sharing growth loop

Reddit content was already everywhere on the internet—but without attribution. Screenshots stripped context, URLs looked sketchy, and the platform had no recognizable off-platform presence. Seven-figure daily sharing volume, completely unoptimized.

As the first designer on this team, I stood up design workflows, established cross-functional processes, and framed sharing as a growth loop I owned end-to-end. The goal: make proper sharing channels faster and more appealing than a screenshot, so attribution and brand travel with every piece of content that leaves the platform.

Understanding both sides of sharing

I partnered with UXR on a longitudinal, foundational study that ran the length of this project—spanning four cohorts (new users, browsing users, searching visitors, and regulars) across social behavior on pseudonymous platforms, privacy perception, and sharing motivations. The study shaped every stage of the framework.

Mapping motivation, inhibition, and outside perception

Our team’s core product questions shaped UXR’s study design—what motivates sharing on a pseudonymous platform, what inhibits it, and how do non-users perceive Reddit content when they encounter it in the wild? The answers built the behavioral model that informed every design decision in this project.

Reducing risk and effort

The research surfaced an asymmetry the work had to address: senders saw sharing as a risk to manage; recipients saw shared content as effort, not value. Every downstream design decision worked to reduce both.

We tested the existing sharing experience to identify breakdowns, then tested what we’d designed with the same participants to validate whether we’d addressed the actual problems. We also user-tested different preview formats with non-Redditors to understand what made sense, what didn’t, and what they’d expect after clicking.

Three principles for the growth loop

The growth loop only works if each leg pulls its weight — sharing has to be easy, shared content has to feel enticing, and sharing has to feel trustworthy. Those three design principles became the three pillars of the work: Streamline Sharing, Simplify Engagement, and Establish Trust.

01 Streamline Sharing

Sharing had no dedicated infrastructure—no share button on posts or in the feed, no consistent controls across surfaces. I scoped this into three discrete launches—contextual entry points, overflow standardization, screenshot interception—each shipped independently so we could measure impact before committing to the next.

Standardize the share menu

Every overflow button in the app looked different—inconsistent across every surface. I designed a standardized overflow sheet with share controls at the top, then partnered with design systems and content design to establish a repeatable action hierarchy that held across surfaces with different tool sets.

Channel Existing Behavior

Sharing was buried in overflow menus—invisible at the moments users were most likely to want it. I mapped contextual triggers across the post detail page and feed, then surfaced controls directly at those points.

Even with contextual entry points in place, users defaulted to screenshots because they were fast and certain. I designed a capture-and-share path that intercepted the screenshot gesture and channeled it into a branded, attributable flow. Instead of fighting the instinct, I built on top of it.

02 Simplify Engagement

Wherever Reddit content appeared off-platform, it needed to be consistent, attributable, and enticing. I designed previews as a first introduction to the platform—a learning tool that established what Reddit is before anyone ever landed on it.

Building brand awareness with previews

I worked with OrangeRed—Reddit’s external branding team—to negotiate the core brand components previews needed to carry. OrangeRed favored rich illustrative concepts; design systems pushed for scalable UI. The resolution: model previews off familiar Reddit platform patterns, injecting brand in high-visibility spots—upvotes and downvotes are brand elements too, not just the logo—so the system carried recognition without sacrificing scalability across six-plus platforms.

Designing first impressions off-platform

I partnered with design systems to build a preview component system that scaled across six-plus platforms without per-platform redesigns. Upvotes and comment counts served double duty—social proof to entice clicks, and education about how the platform works. Reddit’s logo anchored brand recognition for first-time viewers.

Creating previews for text posts

Text posts—the majority of Reddit content—had no thumbnails at all. Our first attempt generated full desktop-page screenshots, which were illegible at mobile preview scale. Rather than pushing for a larger preview surface, I redesigned what to show—working with engineering to strip chrome, isolate the text body and title, and tune truncation until we had crisp, legible cards that scaled for both long and short form.

03 Establish Trust

Streamlining flows and improving previews addressed the mechanics—but volume still wasn’t moving. The real friction wasn’t speed or aesthetics. On a pseudonymous platform, privacy was the core barrier to sharing.

Redesigning the share sheet

I replaced the iOS default—a text list users scrolled through blindly—with a visual icon grid that surfaced each user’s most-used channels first. The grid reordered dynamically based on sharing history, with considerations for the cold-start default and the threshold for promoting a channel.

Closing the trust gap

The redesigned share sheet shipped—and users still hesitated. Research showed the barrier wasn’t speed—it was that users on a pseudonymous platform couldn’t tell what information would travel with the share. I added a live preview of exactly what the recipient would see, plus an explicit privacy disclosure confirming the sender’s username wouldn’t be exposed—the preview became the trust mechanism.

Privacy on the profile

The trust lens extended past sharing. Working with the Profile team, I pulled privacy controls onto the profile itself — a single surface where a user could see what others saw of them, and remove anything they didn’t want public. Privacy stopped being something users had to hunt for; it lived where they already were.

SHR-16 — profile privacy (profile view with privacy controls surfaced inline) — pending Figma mock

The growth loop in motion

Reddit went from zero sharing infrastructure to optimized, branded experiences across every major channel. Inbound traffic from shared content increased, attributed content appeared more frequently off-platform, and the preview component system became the foundation for a public embed API. The privacy-as-growth-lever framework shaped how growth teams across Reddit approached user control as a distribution strategy.

The preview component system also extended to publishers — a public embed API that built on the same work, making the attributed path easier than screenshotting.
